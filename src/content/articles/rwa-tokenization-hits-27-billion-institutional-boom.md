The tokenized real-world asset (RWA) market has crossed a threshold that few predicted would arrive this fast. In April 2026, on-chain RWAs surpassed $27.6 billion in total value, a fourfold jump from where the market stood just three years ago. What was once a niche experiment is now a full-blown institutional trend.

## From Pilot to Pipeline

Back in 2022, the RWA market was a footnote. A handful of projects were tokenizing invoices, real estate fractions, and private credit, mostly as proof-of-concept. By early 2023, the numbers were still modest -- under $5 billion. But the pace of growth since then has been striking.

From $5 billion in 2022 to $26.4 billion by March 2026 represents roughly 380% growth in three years. That kind of trajectory does not happen without institutional capital following it.

A joint report by RedStone, Gauntlet, and RWA.xyz put it plainly: "Asset tokenization has decisively transitioned from experimental pilots to scaled institutional adoption." The report, covering 2024 and 2025, found that the shift was not theoretical. Real capital was moving onto real blockchains.

## Government Bonds Are the Engine

The fastest-growing segment within RWAs is tokenized government bonds, specifically US Treasuries. Tokenized T-bills now represent over $10 billion of the total market on their own.

The appeal is not complicated. Treasuries pay a yield. Putting them on-chain makes them composable, programmable, and usable as collateral inside DeFi protocols. Instead of holding idle USDC in a wallet, a user or protocol can hold tokenized T-bills, earn the risk-free rate, and still deploy that capital as needed.

BlackRock's BUIDL fund leads this category with roughly $2.9 billion in assets under management. The catch: a $5 million minimum buy-in. That threshold locks out retail entirely and signals who this product is actually built for -- sovereign wealth funds, family offices, and institutional treasuries.

## Ripple Joins the Stack

One of the more notable recent moves came from Ripple, which announced a partnership with Kyobo Life Insurance to enable Korea's first tokenized government bond settlement via Ripple Custody. The deal puts XRP Ledger infrastructure at the center of a real sovereign debt workflow, not a demo.

Korea is not alone. Multiple governments in the Asia-Pacific region are actively exploring tokenized bond issuance, and private custodians are racing to provide the rails.

## Which Chains Are Winning?

Ethereum still dominates. It hosts over 60% of all tokenized RWA value, largely because institutional players trust its security track record and its established DeFi ecosystem provides ready liquidity venues.

But Ethereum is not the only game. Stellar, Polygon, Avalanche, and Solana each have meaningful RWA footprints. Stellar, in particular, has carved out a niche in cross-border payment settlements and tokenized securities. Solana is gaining ground through speed and low fees, which matter for high-frequency settlement use cases.

## The Retail Question

The current RWA boom is almost entirely an institutional story. BlackRock requires $5 million to participate. Most tokenized bond products come with accredited investor requirements. Private credit tokenization is even more restricted.

There are exceptions. Some platforms are tokenizing US real estate with entry points as low as $50. But these are early-stage products with limited liquidity and high risk.

The promise of democratizing access to high-quality assets through tokenization has not yet materialized for ordinary users. That gap between the narrative and the reality is worth watching.

## What Comes Next

McKinsey has projected the RWA tokenization market could reach $2 trillion by 2030. That number sounds large until you realize global bond markets alone exceed $100 trillion.

The infrastructure buildout is accelerating. Custodians, asset managers, and blockchain networks are all competing for position. The question is no longer whether real-world assets will move on-chain. It is which chains, which custodians, and which regulatory frameworks will define how they get there.

For now, the institutions are moving first. Retail will follow when the minimums come down and the user experience catches up.
