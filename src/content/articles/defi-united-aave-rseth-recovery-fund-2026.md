When a $292 million exploit rocked KelpDAO last week, the immediate instinct across crypto Twitter was grim: "DeFi is dead." But within 48 hours, something unexpected happened. Instead of fragmentation and fingerpointing, the sector's largest protocols began pooling resources to fix the damage together.

The effort is called DeFi United, and it may be the most consequential test yet of whether decentralized finance can act like an industry when it matters.

## What Happened

On April 18, attackers linked to North Korea's Lazarus Group exploited a misconfigured LayerZero bridge used by KelpDAO, draining roughly 116,500 rsETH (about $292 million) from the protocol's Ethereum mainnet escrow. The stolen tokens were immediately deposited as collateral across Aave, Compound, and Euler, and the attacker borrowed an estimated $236 million in WETH and wrapped staked ETH against them.

The result: rsETH, a liquid restaking token that was supposed to represent one-to-one backed ether, was now severely undercollateralized. Protocols holding rsETH as collateral faced a shortfall. Aave alone saw around $10 billion in deposits withdrawn as users fled, pushing DeFi TVL to its lowest level in a year.

## The Coalition Response

Rather than leaving KelpDAO to fend for itself, Aave service providers put forward a recovery vehicle they called DeFi United. The goal is straightforward: collectively lend enough ETH into a rescue fund to restore rsETH's backing, giving users confidence that the token can be redeemed and preventing a broader liquidation cascade.

The contributions have been substantial. Aave founder Stani Kulechov personally committed 5,000 ETH. Mantle's treasury proposed lending up to 30,000 ETH to Aave DAO for the effort. Lido Finance submitted a governance proposal on April 24 to contribute up to 2,500 stETH. Other protocols are still deliberating, but the momentum is real.

## Why This Matters Beyond One Hack

DeFi has had big hacks before. What is new here is the coordinated response. Historically, a protocol getting drained meant angry users, a crashing token, and everyone else quietly distancing themselves from the blast radius. The incentive was always to let the failure be isolated.

DeFi United flips that logic. The argument Aave is making, implicitly, is that a major undercollateralized token circulating across the ecosystem creates systemic risk for everyone. If rsETH loses its peg on Arbitrum and Optimism and Base, it does not stay contained. It spreads as bad collateral through every lending market that accepted it.

That framing, treating a competitor's crisis as a shared problem, is a notable shift.

## The Governance Challenge

Whether DeFi United actually succeeds depends on governance votes at each participating protocol. Lido's 2,500 stETH proposal is still pending community approval. Mantle's contribution faces a similar process. Aave DAO itself needs to ratify the deployment of the recovery fund.

This creates a coordination problem that decentralized protocols are not well designed to solve quickly. Governance timelines measured in days become a liability when speed matters. Arbitrum's Security Council already moved faster, unilaterally freezing over 30,000 ETH tied to the exploit, highlighting the practical power of centralized emergency mechanisms even within ostensibly decentralized systems.

## What Institutions Are Watching

JPMorgan noted in a report published April 23 that persistent security flaws and stagnant TVL continue to weigh on DeFi's institutional appeal. The bank acknowledged that any entity solving these security problems is likely to unlock the next major wave of institutional growth.

DeFi United does not solve the underlying bridge vulnerability problem. It does not change the fact that April 2026 is already tracking as the worst month for crypto hacks since early 2025, with over $600 million lost. But it offers a proof of concept for something the sector has lacked: an informal mutual-aid structure that activates when a major protocol fails.

Whether that is enough to move the needle on institutional confidence depends on whether rsETH gets fully restored, and how long it takes. For now, the fact that the industry is even attempting a coordinated rescue is worth noting. That was not a given.
