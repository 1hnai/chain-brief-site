Stripe's blockchain venture is having a moment. Tempo, the payments-focused blockchain co-founded by Stripe and backed by crypto VC firm Paradigm, has spent the past week stacking partnerships at a rate that few blockchain projects manage even in a full year.

The network formally launched in March 2026 after disclosing a $500 million funding round that valued it at $5 billion. But the real action is happening now, as Tempo pivots from launch announcements to actual commercial deployments.

## DoorDash Joins the Network

The most eye-catching addition is DoorDash. The food delivery giant is integrating Tempo to offer stablecoin payouts to merchants and delivery partners, giving gig workers and small restaurants a faster, cheaper alternative to traditional bank transfers. The integration runs through Stripe's existing merchant infrastructure, meaning businesses already using Stripe can opt into stablecoin settlements without reworking their payment stack.

DoorDash is not alone. Coastal Community Bank, OnePay, Felix, Fifth Third Bank, and Howard Hughes Holdings have all announced plans to bring payment operations onto Tempo. For a blockchain that launched less than two months ago, that is a notable roster.

## Visa and Standard Chartered Become Validators

Beyond the merchant side, Tempo has been building out its validator set with names that carry serious institutional weight. Visa signed on as an anchor validator in mid-April, followed shortly by Zodia Custody, the crypto custodian majority-owned by Standard Chartered.

Validators on Tempo are responsible for confirming transactions and maintaining network integrity. Having Visa in that role is meaningful in two ways: it gives Tempo credibility among traditional finance players who remain skeptical of permissionless chains, and it connects the network to Visa's existing relationships with banks and card issuers worldwide.

## The Stablecoin Advisory Play

On April 21, Tempo took another step by launching a formal stablecoin advisory operation. The unit is designed to help businesses and financial institutions identify real use cases for stablecoin payments and offers what it calls forward-deployed engineers, meaning Tempo staff who embed with clients to handle technical integration directly.

This is a playbook borrowed from enterprise software sales, and it signals that Tempo is not just building infrastructure but actively trying to own the go-to-market layer for business stablecoin adoption. Rather than waiting for companies to figure out how to plug in, Tempo is sending people to do it with them.

## Why Tempo Is Different

Most blockchain projects aimed at payments have struggled to gain traction outside of crypto-native users. Tempo's approach is different in a few key ways.

First, Stripe's distribution. Stripe already processes hundreds of billions of dollars in payments annually and has deep integrations with merchants across the globe. Tempo is essentially the blockchain layer underneath existing Stripe infrastructure, which means adoption does not require convincing businesses to switch payment providers.

Second, the Machine Payments Protocol. Tempo launched earlier this year with a built-in protocol that lets AI agents make and receive payments autonomously. As companies deploy more AI-driven workflows, the ability to pay for compute, data, and services programmatically, without human sign-off on each transaction, becomes a real operational need. Tempo is positioning itself as the settlement layer for that future.

Third, stablecoin timing. The regulatory environment for stablecoins in the US is finally becoming clearer, with legislation moving through Congress that would create a proper licensing framework. Companies that have been waiting on the sidelines are now moving, and Tempo is in place to catch that wave.

## What to Watch

The obvious next question is whether Tempo's validator set and merchant partners translate into meaningful transaction volume. Stablecoin adoption in B2B payments has been talked about for years but has struggled to move beyond niche corridors. DoorDash's gig economy payouts are a concrete test case, one where the value proposition, speed and lower fees versus ACH transfers, is clear.

If Tempo can demonstrate that stablecoin settlement actually reduces friction and cost at scale, the network effect from its Stripe connection could accelerate fast. If it stays mostly theoretical, it joins a long list of ambitious payments blockchain projects that never quite reached escape velocity.

For now, the partnership momentum is real. Watch the transaction data when Tempo starts publishing it.
