After six bruising months, Bitcoin is finally staging a recovery that traders can believe in. As April draws to a close, BTC is on track for its best monthly performance in over a year, climbing more than 13% from its early-month lows near $74,000 to test resistance above $77,000. The turnaround is being driven by a combination of returning institutional capital, a booming stablecoin supply, and renewed confidence in spot ETF products.

## A Long Road Back

The context matters. Crypto markets logged their longest consecutive monthly losing streak since 2018, with Bitcoin posting declines from October 2025 through February 2026. The selloff was broad and deep, with macro headwinds, rising real yields, and a wave of profit-taking from late-cycle buyers all weighing on prices.

March offered a brief respite, but the real recovery came in April. Even a major DeFi shock, the $292 million KelpDAO exploit that rocked the broader market mid-month, failed to derail Bitcoin's upward momentum. BTC bounced back above $76,000 within 48 hours of that event, a show of resilience that surprised many analysts.

## The ETF Flywheel

The clearest driver of this month's rally is spot ETF demand. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) has been a consistent magnet for capital, with US-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs recording approximately $996 million in net inflows in a single recent week alone. That figure represents one of the biggest weekly inflow totals since the products launched in January 2024.

Strategy, the corporate bitcoin treasury vehicle formerly known as MicroStrategy, executed its third-largest Bitcoin purchase on record during the month. The company continues to add to a stockpile now exceeding 760,000 BTC, reinforcing the narrative that corporate adoption at scale is not slowing down.

The cumulative weight of these inflows is significant. ETF products have fundamentally changed the demand structure for Bitcoin. Unlike retail spot buyers who often sell into strength, institutional ETF allocations tend to be sticky, tied to portfolio rebalancing mandates rather than short-term price targets.

## Liquidity Is Back

Another underappreciated factor is stablecoin supply. Tether's USDT has grown to nearly $150 billion in circulation, a record high. Historically, rapid USDT supply expansion has preceded crypto rallies, as it signals fresh capital sitting on the sidelines ready to deploy into risk assets. The $5 billion USDT growth seen in April alone is among the largest monthly expansions ever recorded.

Combined with recovering on-chain activity and rising open interest across derivatives markets, the liquidity backdrop looks markedly better than it did in Q4 2025.

## What Comes Next

Traders are now watching two key levels. The $79,000 zone has acted as a ceiling for several recent attempts. A clean break above it would open up a run toward the $80,000 to $84,000 range that analysts widely cite as the next meaningful resistance cluster.

The risk to this thesis is that Bitcoin is entering this zone after a sharp run-up, with some derivatives metrics showing cooling momentum. Open interest has dipped even as price has climbed, suggesting some traders are taking profit rather than adding exposure.

There is also the broader macro picture to consider. If US interest rates remain elevated and risk appetite fades in equity markets, crypto could face renewed headwinds regardless of the structural demand from ETFs.

## The Bigger Picture

April 2026 is likely to be remembered as the month Bitcoin decoupled from the doom loop of DeFi hacks, regulatory uncertainty, and macro fear that dominated the prior two quarters. Whether the recovery extends will depend heavily on ETF flow data heading into May and whether macro conditions stay supportive.

For now, the numbers speak clearly. Bitcoin is up 13% in a month, ETF inflows are back at near-record pace, and institutional conviction appears to be strengthening. That is a very different story than the one most market participants were telling in January.
